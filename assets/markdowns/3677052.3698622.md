# ABSTRACT

Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have achieved considerable success across a spectrum of computer vision tasks, with applications ranging from healthcare to automated driving.Recent literature has also explored its potential utility in trading and risk management within the finance industry.Despite their versatility, CNNs are substantially constrained by their data-hungry nature. The lack of well-labeled image datasets poses a major challenge to the widespread adoption of CNNs in financial machine learning research across academia and industry.To address these concerns, this work presents Generative-CNN,a novel approach that utilizes a generative adversarial network (GAN) to synthetically generate images to enhance the performance of a CNN with applications in trading.

# 1INTRODUCTION

Since their inception, convolutional neural networks have achieved significant success across various fields. Their applications have ranged across image classification, object detection, image segmentation,and a variety of other tasks.The versatility of convolutional neural networks has enabled their application across a spectrum of domains,ranging from healthcare to automated driving[18,21, 27].

In recent years, researchers have also explored the possibility of applying convolutional neural networks to solving machine learning problems in finance with proposed applications in trading and risk management [14,17].One key application of convolutional neural networks has been in enabling the potential automation of technical analysis [1o].Technical analysis isa form of financial analysis centered around analyzing repeating patterns in financial charts in order to better predict market trend reversals [6,8].

Candestick charts are one of the key charting tools used by technical analysts to analyse these repeating market patterns. Candlestick charts are essentially box-plots that visually represent the 4 key price points for a given entity (stock or index) for a given time-frame (ranging from seconds to weeks).The 4 key price points comprise the opening price,the high price during the period, the low price during the period,and the closing price.Ploting a sequence of candlesticks across a wider time-horizon can effectively paint a telling picture of the current market sentiment by visually explaining the relative dominance of various players (bears and bulls) in the market.Candlesticks can also visually explain the underlying volatility of the given stock or index [9].

Quite naturally, technical analysts are reliant on visual cognition to identify and interpret these repeating trading patterns represented by sequences of candlestick charts.Since convolutional neural networks are exceedingly good at visual learning, they can serve as excellent proxies for human technical analysts,who rely on their eyes to analyze these patterns [2,13,24]. Constructing a convolutional neural network trained on images of prevalently used trading patterns can provide an efficient solution for automating technical analysis [11].

Across industry and academia,research on applying convolutional neural networks to identifying trading patterns has grown at aconsistent pace over the years. On expected lines,CNNs have generally delivered impressive results across various areas in technical analysis and trading [26]. One major challenge faced by researchers in the financial machine learning landscape has revolved around the lack of established image datasets of trading patterns or other visual representations employed in the finance industry. This imposes a significant constraint on the scalability of future research on CNNs in finance.

Most researchers have had to either collect images manually or rely on proprietary visual representation algorithms to generate sufficient loads of image data to efficiently train and test CNNs applied on problems pertaining to trading (and other aspects in the broader financial research landscape)[19].This has served as a significant botteneck holding back further research on applying CNNs to trading.

Synthetic data generation can offera versatile and scalable solution to help overcome this bottleneck [12,16].Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)are a class of neural networks consisting of 2 parts- generator and discriminator,which compete against one another to generate the most optimal output [7].The generator's goal is to generate synthetic data indistinguishable from real data.While,the discriminator's goal is to effectively differentiate between the two.

By building a sequential setup of a GAN and a CNN,we can effectively build a pipeline consisting of a generative model (GAN, in this case) combined with a data-hungry classifier (CNN,in this case).We propose this model as Generative-CNN in this work.

# 3DATA COLLECTION

As reiterated earlier,research onapplying CNNs to finance has often been significantly constrained by the lack of well-organized image datasets.Data scarcity is one of the main constraints inhibiting the potential expansion of applied CNN research in finance.Naturally, our first objective was to focus on buildinga quality image dataset to overcome this constraint.

There are close to 60-70 different candlestick patterns that are widely used by technical analysts to predict trend reversals in various markets.Considering the scope of our current research, we decided to restrict our focus to 2 patterns exclusively.In order to achieve proportional representation of both types of reversals,it was imperative that the two classes each represent one type of reversal: bullish and bearish.

Bullish reversals are instances where a market ina downward trend reverses and starts rebounding in an upward trajectory. Bearish reversals,on the other hand,refer to the converse,where a stock in an upward trajectory peaks and starts falling in a downward trend post the peak.

# 3.1Head and Shoulders Pattern

To represent the bearish reversal class,we choose a complex candlestick pattern called the "Head and Shoulders"pattern.The "Head and Shoulders" pattern is a multi-candle pattern. It must contain a minimum of 7 candles. Usually on an average,it can contain close to 20 to 25 candles.

The "Head and Shoulders" pattern typically consists of a left shoulder, the head,and a right shoulder.

At the "Left Shoulder",the price rises to a peak and then declines. It forms the first peak in the pattern.

At the "Head",the price rises again,creatinga higher peak than the left shoulder before declining again.This is the highest peak in the pattern.

Figure 1: Head & Shoulders Pattern. The pattern indicates a potential reversal from a bullish to a bearish trend.

Atthe "Right Shoulder",the price rises again,but this time it does not reach the height of the head before declining.This forms the second shoulder.

The "Head and Shoulders"pattern indicates a potential reversal from a bullish trend to a bearish trend.When the price breaks below the neckline,it suggests the trend is reversing,and the asset may experience further declines.

# 3.2Inverse Head and Shoulders Pattern

To represent the bullish reversal class,we choose the inverse of the "Head and Shoulders"pattern.The "Inverse Head and Shoulders"pattern is also a multi-candle pattern.It must also containa minimum of 7 candles.And similarly,on an average,it can contain close to 20 to 25 candles.

The "Inverse Head and Shoulders" pattern typically consists of a left shoulder (left trough),the head (central valley),and a right shoulder (right trough).

At the "Left Shoulder",the price drops to a trough and then rises. It forms the first trough in the pattern.

At the "Head",the price declines again,creatinga lower trough than the left shoulder before rising again.This is the lowest valley in the pattern.

Atthe "Right Shoulder",the price declines again,but this time it does not reach the depth of the head before rising.This forms the second shoulder.

The "Inverse Head and Shoulders"pattern indicates a potential reversal from a bearish trend to a bullish trend.When the price breaks above the neckline,it suggests the trend is reversing,and the asset may experience further rises.

# 3.3Workflow

As part of the data collection process,we manually collect $2 0 0 \ \mathrm { i m } \cdot$ ages of the "Head and Shoulders"pattern and 2oo of the "Inverse Head and Shoulders" pattern.We use this collection of 400 images across 2 classes to build the initial image dataset.In order to maintain consistency,we restrict our selection range of underlying stocks to approximately 10o high-volume stocks traded in the US markets. We use "Green-Red"color coding for the candles.Green candles represent higher closing price days (days when the stock closed higher than the day's opening price).And red candles represent the lower closing price days (days when the stock closed lower than the day's opening price).We also superimpose a 10-day exponential moving average line over the candlesticks to trace the trajectory of the patterns.The collected images are standardized by following these charting conventions consistently.

# 4CONVOLUTIONAL NEURAL NETWORK

Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are specialised neural networks primarily used for visual learning tasks. They have applications in tasks such as feature extraction, hierarchical learning, pattern recognition,and dimensionality reduction. CNNs are structured with multiple layers that reduce the dimensions of image data and learn hierarchical patterns present in them. The key characteristic of CNNs are the alternating convolutional and pooling layers.Convolutional layers function as feature selection layers. While,pooling layers reduce the dimensionality of data.There are 2 types of pooling layers.Max pooling layers retain the maximum values for each region in the feature map and aid in identifying edges.Average pooling layers on the other hand, retain the average of the clusters present in the different regions of the feature map. These capabilities make CNNs ideally suited for tasks involving visual pattern recognition.

Since technical analysis is primarily a visual learning task, CNNs can potentially serve as excellent proxies for human technical analysts who rely on their eyes to analyze the recurring patterns.

The Inception V3 model is 48 layers deep and contains a mix of multiple convolutional, pooling (max and average),and fully connected layers.

# 5GENERATIVE ADVERSARIAL NETWORK

Generative adversarial networks (GANs) are a class of machine learning frameworks developed in 2014.They consistof 2 neural networks,that compete against each other in the form of a zerosum game,where one agent's gain is another's loss.The 2 neural networks are: the generator and the discriminator.

The generator generates synthetic data,aiming to create synthetic data thatis indistinguishable fromreal-world data.The discriminator,on the other hand,aims to distinguish between the two in order to pick out the synthetic data.The generator tries to replicate realistic data,while the discriminator tries to get better at identifying synthetic data from real-world data.Eventually, the generator,optimized by competing with the discriminator, starts to produce high-quality synthetic data which perfectly mimics its real-wold counterparts.

GANs have been widely used for a variety of tasks such as image generation, video generation, text-to-image synthesis, etc.They are particularly useful in situations where acquiring large amounts of labeled data is challenging.

Naturally, GANs can play a critical role in enhancing the performance of CNNs used in financial pattern recognition (and image classification) by alleviating the key constraint of data scarcity (labeled image datasets in finance).

# 5.1 Deep Convolutional GAN (DCGAN)

In this work,we have employed the "Deep Convolutional GAN (DCGAN)" model to function as the synthetic image generator [20,28].Deep Convolutional GANs (DCGANs)are a type of Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) that utilize deep convolutional networks for both the generator and discriminator.They are particularly effective for generating high-quality images and are characterized by their use of convolutional layers instead of fully connected layers in the networks (generator and discriminator).DCGANs are also distinguished by their ability to take images as input.DCGANs can essentially be trained directly using images to generate synthetic images that replicate the characteristics of the input images. These attributes make DCGANs an excellent choice,to synthetically generate candlestick images for CNN classification.

The Generator network $G$ is designed to produce synthetic images from random noise.It consists of several transposed convolutional layers,which upsample the input noise vector to generate images of the desired size.The architecture of the Generator is as follows:

· Input: Random noise vector z ∈ R100 · Transposed Convolution Layers: To progressively increase the spatial dimensions of the input.

·Activation Function: ReLU for intermediate layers and Tanh for the output layer. · Output: Synthetic image of size $6 4 \times 6 4 \times 3$ （204

The Discriminator network $D$ is responsible for distinguishing between real and synthetic images.It is composed of several convolutional layers followed by a fully connected layer. The architecture of the Discriminator is as follows:

· Input: Image of size $6 4 \times 6 4 \times 3$   
· Convolutional Layers: To extract features and downsample the input image.   
·Activation Function: LeakyReLU for intermediate layers and Sigmoid for the output layer.   
· Output: Probability score indicating whether the image is real or fake.

# 6GENERATIVE-CNN

Finally,we arrive at Generative-CNN,an integrated pipeline with a sequential setup of a GAN and a CNN. The integration of images obtained from real-world sources along with synthetically generated ones,presents a novel approach to enhancing the performance of data-hungry frameworks such as CNNs.Generative-CNN capitalizes on the strengths of both sources of data,by leveraging the diversity inherent in real-world images,while also capitalizing on the scalability offered by synthetically-generated images.

We build an integrated datasetby combining a random sample of 70 real-world images (from the foundational dataset) with 840 synthetically-generated images (from the GAN's output).This integrated dataset combines the strengths of both sources of data.It blends the diversity offered by the real-world images with the sheer scalable numbers offered by the GAN's output (to offset the data scarcity constraint).We feed this data to the "Inception V3" CNN, to evaluate its expected enhanced performance.

# 7.4 Time Optimization

The constraint of data scarcity plays a critical role in impacting the time taken to build any initial dataset for CNN research in finance.In this work,the initial dataset of 4oo real-world images was constructed by manually collecting screenshots of the 2 patterns,visualised using a stock charting software.It takes close to 6 hours (350 minutes) to collect a set of 70 images and the time taken scales up linearly with the increase in quantity.For 40o images,it takes more than 33 hours (2oo0 minutes) to complete collecting the dataset.

In contrast,using a GAN to generate synthetic replicas of the real-world images drastically reduces the time invested in the data collection and preprocessing phase. The GAN when run using a cloud-based GPU,roughly takes around 3-4 minutes to generate 840 synthetic images of the 2 patterns.This drastically optimizes the time invested in the data collection phase.In addition to superior performance metrics (loss and accuracy),"Generative-CNN"also provides an innovative solution to the challenge of time optimization during data collection for CNN research in finance.

# 8CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

In this research,we explored the efficacy of utilizing Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) to generate synthetic data and enhance the performance of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) employed in financial pattern recognition. Our findings can be summarized as follows:

# 8.1 Data Scarcity and Time Optimization

Data scarcity in financial pattern recognition significantly impacts the training time and overall performance of CNN models.Manually collecting a dataset of 400 real-world images is time-intensive, requiring approximately 33 hours.In contrast, employing a GAN to generate synthetic replicas of real-world images significantly reduces data collection time,requiring only 3-4 minutes to produce 840 synthetic images.This represents a drastic reduction in time investment and highlights the efficiency of GANs in data augmentation tasks.

# 8.3Innovative Solution

The "Generative-CNN" approach provides superior performance metrics and offers an innovative solution to the challenge of data scarcity in financial machine learning.By leveraging synthetic data, the model can generalize better,reduce overfitting,and ensure more stable learning.Building upon these findings,this research opens new frontiers in financial machine learning, paralleling successes seen in the biomedical field.

(3）Real-time Applications: Deploying the Generative-CNN model in real-time trading environments to assess its practical applicability and performance under live market conditions.   
(4) Cross-domain Applications: Investigating the potential of applying the Generative-CNN framework to other domains that face similar data scarcity issues,such as medical imaging and environmental monitoring.

By addressing these areas, we can continue to push the boundaries of applied machine learning research in finance and beyond.
