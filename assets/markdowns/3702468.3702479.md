# Abstract

Time series classification is crucial in fields such as healthcare, finance,and industrial processes,but it faces challenges like temporal data ordering,class imbalance,noise,and limited data.This research explores data augmentation techniques to improve classifcation performance,focusing on the MiniRocket classifier across 85 UCR datasets.The study identifies conditions under which augmentation techniques, like wDBA,enhance accuracy, though overall performance may vary.A dataset-specific approach is essential for effective augmentation. The research also examines the impact of augmentation on datasets with different characteristics,providing insights into when specific strategies are most beneficial.Future work includes optimizing augmentation methods for specific domains, exploring advanced techniques like generative models,and validating results on real-world datasets to ensure practical applicability.

# 1 Introduction

Time series data is a collection of observations obtained through repeated measurements over time.It is used in various domains suchas weather forecasting,healthcare,finance,and the Internet of Things (IoT). Time series data can be found almost everywhere, and with advancements in technology like 5G and IoT,we are constantly generating and collecting massive amounts of time series data. Time series classification involves assigning a predefined class label to each input of time series data, enabling us to categorize and label this data effectively. This classification is essential in various domains and applications.For example,in healthcare,itaids in diagnosing diseases based on patient data, such as electrocardiogram (ECG)analysis [1] or sleep stage classification.In finance,it helps analyze financial data,such as stock market trends or detect credit card fraud.In industrial settings,it is used to monitor and control industrial processes, such as predictive maintenance or anomaly detection.

Moreover,with the increasing complexity and volume of time series data,the need for accurate and efficient classification methods has become more critical. The proliferation of IoT devices, for instance,has led to an explosion of sensor data,which must beanalyzed in real-time to provide actionable insights.In smart cities,time series classification can optimize traffc flow, manage energy consumption,and enhance public safety by predicting and responding to various urban phenomena.In environmental science, it can be used to monitor climate change,track wildlife movements and manage natural resources more effectively.Furthermore, the application of time series classification in personalized healthcare has gained traction,allowing for the monitoring of chronic diseases, personalized treatment plans,and early detection of health anomalies,thereby improving patient outcomes and reducing healthcare costs.

Time series classification presents distinct challenges compared to traditional classification tasks.Unlike tabular or image data, time series data has an inherent temporal order,where the sequence of data points is crucial to its meaning. Class imbalance is a common issue,often skewing models toward the majority class,as seen in electrocardiogram classification,where most data represent healthy individuals.The high dimensionality and dynamic nature of time series data further complicate classification,requiring advanced models like RNNs and CNNs to capture both local and global patterns.Interpretability is critical,especially in fields like healthcare and finance,where understanding the model's decisions is as vital as accuracy.Additionally,noise from sources like low-quality sensors can obscure patterns,while limited data availability,such as in rare disease cases,hinders effective classification.

In recent years,deep learning algorithms have gained popularity in various domains,including time series classification.However, deep learning models typically require a substantial amount of training data to achieve optimal performance.Consequently, the limited availability of data can hinder the effectiveness of deep learning models in such scenarios.In recent years,data augmentation has emerged as an approach to address these challenges and improve the performance of machine models in various domains. Data augmentation involves generating additional synthetic data samples by applying a diverse set of transformations to the existing dataset. Data augmentation is widely used in image classification problems such as cropping,rotating or blurring images to create additional training examples.And it is proven to increase performance of classification models of image classification problems [2].

The application of data augmentation in time series classification, however, is relatively novel and presents its own set of challenges and opportunities.While image data augmentation techniques can often be directly visualized and intuitively understood, time series data augmentation must preserve the temporal relationships and statistical properties of the data to be effective.Techniques such as time warping,scaling,and jittering are commonly used,but their impacts can vary widely depending on the nature of the data and the specific classification task.For instance,time warping might be beneficial for some physiological signals but detrimental for others,depending on how the variations align with the meaningful patterns in the data.

Nevertheless,it is crucial to acknowledge that the implementation of data augmentation does not guarantee an improvement in the performance of machine learning models.As will be demonstrated in the preliminary results of this research work, data augmentation can,at times,impede performance instead.In some cases,excessive or inappropriate data augmentation can lead to model performance degradation. The transformations used to generate additional data samples need to be carefully chosen and tuned. If these transformations do not represent realistic variations of the input data, they can introduce noise or misleading information, which can confuse the model and reduce its ability to generalize well to unseen data. Similarly,if the generated samples do not sufficiently diversify the dataset, they may lead to overfitting,where the model becomes too tailored to the training data and performs poorly on new data. Therefore,while data augmentation has shown effectiveness in many contexts,it should be applied carefully,and its impact should be thoroughly evaluated in each specific use case [3].

The objectives of this research are to evaluate the effectiveness of data augmentation methods,such as time warping,random resampling,in influencing the performance of different time series classification algorithms and datasets.Additionally, this research aims to provide a concise guide for individuals seeking to improve accuracy by augmenting their datasets,offering recommendations on suitable data augmentation techniques.The scope of this work specifically focuses on addressing univariate time series classification problems,exploring the effects of different data augmentation methods on the classification tasks rather than evaluating specific time series classificationalgorithms.

# 2LITERATUREREVIEW

Time series classification algorithms can be categorized based on how they represent and analyze the data [4,5]. Each algorithm category may have different requirements and characteristics,and the effectiveness of data augmentation methods can vary accordingly. Therefore,it is crucial to have a brief understandingof the different categories of classification algorithms to assess their performance when combined with various data augmentation techniques. By gaining insights into each algorithm category,we can better comprehend the potential impact and benefits of data augmentation on time series classification tasks.

· Distance-based: Classification is performed by utilizing specific distance measures tailored for time series data to assess similarities or dissimilarities between series,e.g.,Nearest neighbor (NN) with Dynamic Time Warping [6].   
·Feature-based: Global features are extracted from the time series and passed to a standard classifier, typically following a simple pipeline structure,e.g.,TSFresh[7] and FreshPRINCE [8].   
·Interval-based: Features are derived from selected phasedependent intervals using an ensemble of pipelines, where different intervals capture distinct temporal patterns, e.g., DrCIF[9] and RSTSF[10].   
· Shapelet-based:Discriminatory subseries，known as shapelets,are identified as phase-independent features that serve as the basis for classification,e.g.,RSDT[11] and MrSQM[12].   
·Dictionary-based: Features are constructed by counting the occurrences of repeating patterns in the time series and forming histograms,which are then utilized for classification, e.g.,WEASEL-D [13] and TDE [14].   
Convolution-based: Features are generated by applying convolutions and pooling operations to the time series data, creatinga feature space suitable for classification,e.g., Hydra+MultiROCKET[15,16] MiniROCKET[17].   
Deep learning-based: Classification is accomplished through the utilization of neural network architectures,enabling the model to automatically learn complex patterns and dependencies in the time series data, e.g., InceptionTime [18] and ResNet [19].   
· Hybrid approaches (ensemble): It involves the combination of two or more of the previously mentioned approaches. This integration allows for the utilization of their complementary strengths,resulting in enhanced classification performance. Notable instances of such hybrid approaches include HIVECOTE 2.0 [9] and TS-Chief [20].

In the field of Time Series Classification (TSC),significant strides have been made in algorithmic advancements over the past decade, leading toa substantial growth in related research.The latest review,Bake off redux [5],offers a comprehensive evaluation and experimental analysis of recent time series classification algorithms. The paper compared the performance of multiple algorithms in each category based on 112 UCR dataset [21] and additional 30 datasets to provide less chance of model overfitting.This paper concludes that Hydra-MultiROCKET and HIVE-COTE 2.0 are the two classification models that perform better than any other models.The average accuracy of Hydra-MultiROCKET and HIVE-COTE 2.0 on 142 datasets is 0.884 and 0.892,respectively.

Data augmentation has been extensively researched and applied in various domains beyond time series classification,providing valuable insights and methodologies that can be adapted.For example, in natural language processing (NLP), techniques such as synonym replacement,random insertion,and back-translation have been used to generate diverse training data, enhancing the robustness and performance of text classification models.These methods highlight the importance of creating realistic and varied synthetic data,which is a principle that can be translated to time series data augmentation.

In computer vision, sophisticated augmentation techniques like Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs)and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) have been employed to create high-quality synthetic images. GANs have shown remarkable success in generating realistic images by learning the underlying data distribution. The potential application of such advanced generative models in time series augmentation could open new avenues for research,particularly in creating synthetic data that closely mimics real-world time series patterns.

While data augmentation is well-established in other domains,its application to time series classification is underexplored. Current research lacks comprehensive empirical evaluations of augmentation techniques across different types of time series data and classification algorithms. Furthermore, there is no consensus on which augmentation methods are most effective for specific time series classfication tasks.The following gaps have been identified:

·Lack of Standardized Evaluation: There is a need for standardized benchmarks and evaluation protocols to compare the effectiveness of different data augmentation techniques for time series classification.   
·Effectiveness Across Algorithms: The impact of data augmentation on various classification algorithms has not been thoroughly investigated.   
·Parameter Optimization: Many studies do not explore the optimal parameter settings for augmentation techniques, which can significantly influence their performance.   
·Domain-Specific Adaptations: There is limited research on adapting augmentation techniques to specific domains,such as healthcare or finance,where the nature of time series data can vary significantly.

# 3.2 Data Augmentation

Data augmentation is a widely used technique to enhance the accuracy of models. It has been proven effective in improving the performance of machine learning models, especially for image classification tasks [2].The concept involves generating additional data instances by applying various transformations, thereby increasing the diversity of the training data and providing more examples for the model to learn from.

In the field of image classification, data augmentation is considered a fundamental aspect of model training,particularly when data is sparse, limited, or imbalanced. While the specific methods may vary depending on the application,the underlying principle remains the same:artificially expanding the training data by systematically transforming it. These techniques aim to create additional synthetic data instances that are realistic and relevant to the problem at hand, while still preserving the original data's structural characteristics and label distributions.This not only enables models to be trained on larger datasets but also enhances their robustness and ability to generalize by exposing them to a wider range of data scenarios.

It is important to note that data augmentation techniques can differ significantly based on the type of data and task involved. For instance,in image processing,common augmentation methods include geometric transformations such as rotations,translations, and scaling. In contrast,for structured or tabular data, techniques may involve adding noise,resampling data,or generating synthetic data points.

In their paper [23], the authors propose a taxonomy for time series data augmentation methods.This taxonomy categorizes these methods into three primary hierarchical levels:family,domain, and method.The families of data augmentation methods include transformations,pattern mixing,generative models,and decompositions.Each family is further divided into its respective domains, or major subtypes.

As a result,we select various data augmentation methods based on the different categories as stated in [23] as follows.

· Random Transformation - Magnitude: Magnitude Warping [24] is a technique that warps the magnitude of a time series by multiplying it with a smooth curve generated by a cubic spline. This adds small fluctuations to the data,mimicking realistic variations in the magnitude domain without altering the sequence length.   
·Random Transformation - Time:Window Warping [25] involves perturbing the time steps of a time series using a smooth warping path.This method stretches or compresses time intervals based on a cubic spline,altering the temporal structure while maintaining the original sequence characteristics.   
·Random Transformation- Frequency:Fourier Transformbased [26] data augmentation involves manipulating the frequency components of a time series.By transforming the

time series into the frequency domain,modifications such as noise addition can be applied to the amplitude and phase spectra,and then transformed back to the time domain.

· Random Transformation-Frequency:Short Term Fourier Transform (STFT) [27] is used to convert a time series into a sequence of frequency spectra over short time windows. Data augmentation can then be performed by modifying these localized frequency components,which can capture time-varying spectral information.   
Pattern Mixing - Magnitude:Synthetic Minority Oversampling Technique (SMOTE) [28] generates new synthetic time series data by interpolating between existing samples of under-represented classes. This is achieved by taking the diference between a sample and its nearest neighbor, multiplying by a random value,and adding it to the sample.   
Pattern Mixing - Time:Discriminated Guided Warping (DGW) [29] uses a teacher pattern to time warp a reference pattern based on dynamic time warping (DTW). The teacher pattern is chosen based on its discriminative ability, helping to guide the warping process to generate augmented samples that enhance classification performance.   
· Pattern Mixing - Combined: Weighted Dynamic Time Warping Barycentric Averaging(wDBA) [3o] is a method that createsa weighted average of time series patterns using DTW. The weights can be assigned based on the distance to a reference pattern,ensuring that the generated pattern maintains significant features of the class.   
Decomposition: Double Exponential Smoothing [31] is a decomposition method that separates a time series into trend and level components. It applies smoothing factors to both level and trend, generating new synthetic data by extrapolating these components to create plausible variations.

These descriptions provide a brief overview of each data augmentation technique,highlighting their key characteristics and how they alter time series data for classification tasks.

# 3.3MiniROCKET

MiniRocket is an advanced method for time series classification that builds on the ROCKET (RandOm Convolutional KErnel Transform) framework. ROCKET achieves state-of-the-art accuracy in time series classification by transforming input time series using random convolutional kernels and then using the transformed features to traina linear classifier.It is known for its exceptional speed and accuracy,utilizing global max pooling and Proportion of Positive Values (PPV) pooling to produce features that are then used to train a classifier.Despite its efficiency,ROCKET involves a significant degree of randomness in its kernel selection.

MiniRocket transforms input time series using a small, fixed set of convolutional kernels,making it significantly faster and almost entirely deterministic,while maintaining accuracy close to that of ROCKET.MiniRocket is up to 75 times faster than ROCKET, especially on larger datasets,and it achieves this speed by minimizing the randomness involved in the process,with an optional fully deterministic mode.Despite these optimizations,MiniRocket maintains similar accuracy to ROCKET and is comparable to other state-of-the-art methods for time series classification.

The core mechanism of MiniRocket involves transforming time series data using convolutional kernels. It retains key aspects of ROCKET, such as dilation and PPV pooling.The method is highly efficient, reusing convolution output for multiple features,avoiding multiplications in the convolution operation,and processing all kernels for each dilation almost simultaneously. This results in drastic reductions in computation time.

In terms of performance, MiniRocket excels in both training time and scalability.It can train and test classifiers on large datasets in afraction of the time required by other methods,making it highly suitable for large-scale applications. The method's speed and efficiency make it a practical choice for default use in time series classification tasks across various fields,including financial forecasting,medical diagnostics,and sensor data analysis.MiniRocket represents a significant advancement in time series classification, offering a highly efficient and accurate solution for large-scale applications.

By examining these results,it becomes clear that certain data augmentation techniques can be highly effective in specific contexts, even if their overall average performance appears less promising. This highlights the importance of considering dataset-specific characteristics when selecting data augmentation methods for time series classification tasks.

While data augmentation offers substantial benefits,it also presents several challenges that need to be carefully managed. One significant challenge is ensuring that the augmented data maintains the inherent properties of the original time series.For example,excessive augmentation can introduce unrealistic variations,leading to model overftting or poor generalization on unseen data. This is particularly problematic for datasets with strict temporal dependencies,such as ECG signals,where maintaining the physiological relevance of the data is crucial.

Computational limitations also pose a challenge,especially when dealing with large datasets or complex augmentation methods. Techniques like synthetic minority over-sampling (SMOTE) and dynamic time warping (DTW) barycentric averaging can be computationally intensive,requiring significant processing power and memory. These constraints can limit the scalability of data augmentation strategies, particularly in resource-constrained environments.

Moreover, the selection and tuning of augmentation parameters play a critical role in determining their effectiveness. Inappropriate parameter settings can degrade model performance,as demonstrated in preliminary results where excessive or inappropriate augmentation led to performance degradation. Thus,careful experimentation and validation are essential to identify optimal parameter configurations for each specific dataset and application.

# 5Conclusion and Further Research

This research explored the effectiveness of various data augmentation techniques on time series classification tasks using the MiniRocket classification model. The experiments conducted across multiple datasets from the UCR time series archive provided valuable insights into the impact of data augmentation on classification performance.

The findings revealed that data augmentation could significantly enhance classfication accuracy for certain datasets,especially those characterized by small size,noise,and specific types like simulated and device datasets. However,for datasets such as ECG, data augmentation showed limited effectiveness,indicating that the choice of augmentation techniques should be carefully tailored to the dataset characteristics.

Despite the overall negative average performance of augmentation methods,individual analyses show that techniques like wDBA can significantly improve accuracy for certain datasets. This highlights the need for a dataset-specific approach in time series classification tasks.
