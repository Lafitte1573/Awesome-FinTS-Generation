With the growing infl uence of investor sentiment on market dynamics, sentiment analysis has emerged as an effective tool for enhancing fi nancial forecasting models. This study proposes a diversity-enhanced semi-heterogeneous ensemble forecasting framework that integrates sentiment analysis into the forecasting of stock index returns. A supervised stock market sentiment index set is constructed, in which prior knowledge regarding term importance is integrated into the data augmentation process. This enables higher weights to be assigned to sentiment-related terms with superior predictive capacity, thereby allowing the model to prioritize more informative features and enhance its forecasting performance. A series of diverse base models are generated through the integration of multiple attention-PCA techniques and forecasting algorithms based on variable perturbation strategies. These base models are subsequently combined through a suite of ensemble strategies, forming a semi-heterogeneous ensemble model for forecasting $\mathtt { S 8  P 5 0 0 }$ returns. The experiment results demonstrate that the proposed approaches significantly outperform benchmark methods, with notable improvements in both accuracy and diversity.

# 1. Introduction

The stock market occupies a crucial position within the fi nancial system, significantly affecting not only investors’ behavior but also the stability and growth of the broader economy [1]. Given its inherent volatility, investors often need to evaluate investment risks based on expected future returns and adopt appropriate risk management strategies. Accurate forecasts enable investors to better grasp market changes, reduce investment risks, and improve profitability [2]. By anticipating future returns, investors can select strategies that align with their risk preferences and investment objectives, thereby enhancing asset allocation and portfolio optimization. Moreover, analyzing and modeling stock market data not only supports practical decision-making but also contributes to understanding the mechanisms of price formation and market behavior, providing valuable insights for both fi nancial theory and practice.

The predictability of stock market returns has been a hot research issue [3,4]. The volatility and structural complexity inherent in stock markets make accurate forecasting particularly challenging [5,6]. In the fi eld of stock market forecasting, two principal analytical paradigms are commonly employed: fundamental analysis and technical analysis [7]. Traditional studies have primarily relied on statistical time series models that utilize historical data to forecast stock prices and returns [8,9]. With the emergence of artifi cial intelligence, an increasing number of studies have explored the application of machine learning techniques in this domain, including neural networks [10], decision trees [11], and support vector machine [12]. While these traditional single-model approaches yield valuable insights, their forecasting performance tends to deteriorate markedly under highly dynamic and uncertain market environments [13].

Conventional stock forecasting models often rely solely on technical or fundamental indicators, which may not fully refl ect market perceptions and investor sentiment. However, behavioral fi nance theory suggests that market dynamics are not purely rational, and investor emotions and collective sentiment play a crucial role in price formation [14,15]. Moreover, with the explosive growth of textual data and news coverage, developing methods that can efficiently extract and integrate sentiment information has become increasingly valuable and practically indispensable. This motivates our research, which aims to improve stock forecasting performance by incorporating sentiment analysis.

Recent studies have increasingly utilized textual data to construct predictors of stock returns [16,17]. These predictors are extracted from unstructured textual sources, including fi nancial news, analyst reports, and other related publications [18]. One commonly adopted approach for extracting information from textual data involves the use of a fi xed lexicon [19]. However, to ensure continued relevance and effectiveness, such lexicons must be regularly updated to refl ect evolving vocabulary and changes in the forecasting capacity of existing terms [20]. In the absence of a predefi ned lexicon, the textual data are typically transformed into a high-dimensional time series, where each dimension represents the frequency of a specifi c term appearing in documents over time [21]. Nevertheless, not all terms contribute meaningfully to the forecasting of stock returns. To address this issue, one of the objectives of this study is to identify the most informative and predictive textual variables, achieved through the utilization of supervised data augmentation techniques.

The quality of the dataset plays a critical role in determining the accuracy of forecasting models [22]. To enhance the robustness of stock returns forecasting, data augmentation techniques are employed in this study to enrich and expand the training dataset. Data augmentation is a widely adopted strategy in deep learning to mitigate overfi tting [23]. It involves enriching the dataset by either introducing modifi ed versions of existing data or generating new synthetic samples derived from the original data [24]. This approach has proven particularly effective in the fi eld of natural language processing [25]. In this paper, we propose a supervised data augmentation technique that incorporates term importance during the sampling process. Specifically, predictive terms are assigned higher weights, thereby introducing greater noise for more informative features. This targeted weighting strategy enhances the diversity of the training data and improves the generalization capability on the test data.

The volatility of the stock market arises from their sensitivity to a wide array of infl uencing factors, resulting in substantial complexity and instability [26]. This inherent unpredictability poses significant challenges for stock forecasting, particularly when relying on a single model to capture the rapid and nonlinear fl uctuations of real-time markets [27]. To address these challenges, this study introduces the ensemble forecasting framework, which integrates multiple base models to improve forecasting performance [28]. A homogeneous ensemble refers to the integration of base forecasting models generated by the same algorithm, whereas a heterogeneous ensemble involves combining models derived from distinct learning algorithms [29,30]. To enhance generalization and mitigate overfi tting, diversity among base models is essential. In this context, diversity is defi ned as the degree of dissimilarity among the individual models within the ensemble [31]. Achieving both accuracy and diversity in base models is critical for constructing more robust ensemble forecasting [32]. Accordingly, this paper proposes a semi-heterogeneous ensemble approach for forecasting stock returns, which leverages the complementary strengths of different base models to achieve more accurate and diverse forecasts.

To bridge these gaps, this study proposes a semi-heterogeneous ensemble forecasting framework for ??&?? 500 returns, grounded in sentiment analysis. We construct supervised sentiment indices by incorporating prior knowledge of term importance into the data augmentation process. This allows the model to assign greater weight to more predictive sentiment variables, thereby improving feature relevance and model interpretability. Subsequently, we generate a diverse pool of base models by integrating attentionprincipal component analysis (attention-PCA) with variable perturbation strategies and different forecasting algorithms, which ensure both accuracy and diversity. These models are then aggregated through semi-heterogeneous ensemble mechanisms that combine both homogeneous and heterogeneous structures to balance forecasting robustness and generalization.

The main contributions are threefold. First, this paper proposes a supervised data augmentation technique to construct a sentiment index. Specifically, we introduce the priori knowledge of term importance during the term sampling process and increasing the noise of the data by assigning different weights according to the level of importance. Additionally, higher weights are assigned to more predictive words, allowing for a greater focus on predictor variables with superior predictive capacity. This ultimately improves the forecasting accuracy. The objective is to identify the most relevant input predictor variables by calculating the degree of correlation between the input term variables and the forecasting target. In particular, a variable perturbation-based attention-PCA technique is put forth as a means of identifying the significance of stock return correlates. By measuring the degree of attention between the input term variables and the predictor targets, it is possible to estimate the correlation between stock returns and the frequency of occurrence of various terms.

Second, we construct a pool of base forecasting models with both accuracy and diversity. The attention-PCA technique is employed to extract pivotal information from the sentiment lexicon and construct an array of sentiment indices, thereby enhancing the diversity of the training data. This augments the dataset and facilitates the capacity of the model to generalize more effectively to the test set. Based on the interaction of multiple attention-PCA methods with distinct forecasting algorithms, a multitude of base models is generated. In the generation of individual forecasts, a variety of attention mechanisms are employed to construct diverse sentiment indices, which then interact with different forecasting models to enhance the diversity of the base models.

Third, a semi-heterogeneous ensemble forecasting framework is developed via further interaction of variable disturbance and various forecasting strategies, which includes characteristics of both heterogeneous and homogeneous combinations. Multiple ensemble strategies are applied to integrate the base forecasting models, which further improves the forecasting accuracy and generalization ability of the ensemble model. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed semi-heterogeneous ensemble forecasting method is an effective approach for achieving more accurate forecasting results.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the methodology used in this paper, including attentionPCA approach, base forecasting models and ensemble forecasting approach. Section 3 provides empirical results and discussions. Section 4 is a further analysis of the forecast results. Section 5 concludes this paper.

# 2.1. Base predictors generation mechanism

To construct a robust ensemble model, it is essential to generate base predictors that are both accurate and diverse. This study combines multiple forecasting algorithms with sentiment analysis techniques, ensuring that each base predictor captures distinct aspects of the underlying data patterns. # 2.1.1. Construction of a sentiment lexicon

This study employs the textual data collected monthly from the New York Times (NYT) and the Wall Street Journal (WSJ), selecting only articles in the fi nancial markets category. It is assumed that the content of these newspapers accurately refl ects the concerns of the average investor [33].

In this study, we selected NYT and WSJ as sentiment sources primarily because our forecasting target is the monthly S&P 500 index returns. In contrast, high-frequency sentiment sources like Twitter or Reddit may offer granular and real-time data, but they are often more relevant for high-frequency trading or short-term volatility forecasting. For monthly-level forecasting, news media with structured reporting cycles and in-depth content tend to more accurately capture broader economic and fi nancial sentiment trends that shape long-term investor behavior [34]. NYT and WSJ provide well-researched, professionally written articles that maintain high editorial standards. This ensures lower noise, higher signal quality, and greater objectivity compared to user-generated content on social platforms. These outlets cater primarily to institutional investors, policymakers, and fi nancial professionals, whose behavior often drives the monthly trends of major indices like the S&P 500. Therefore, they serve as a reasonable proxy for infl uential market sentiment. Tetlock suggests that WSJ refl ects readers’ interests [35], which is empirically supported [33].

All articles are initially stored in a corpus, which is a collection of texts. Firstly, the raw texts are subjected to a series of preprocessing steps, removing stop words, hyphens, numbers and words with only two characters, etc. In addition, words are reduced to stems and some specifi c word combinations are also identifi ed. In this paper, an algorithm developed by Straka & Straková [36] is utilized to create a term for the two-word sequences and word collocations are employed to forecast stock returns. Ultimately, the remaining terms (words and binary collocations) are ranked using the Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency (TF-IDF) method [37]. This approach penalizes rare and overly frequent terms and removes all terms ranked 100 or lower.

For the preprocessed lexicon, calculate the frequency of terms mentioned in the articles published in month t. This information is stored in a vector of length $T { \times } 1$ , where $T$ is the number of time points, with each element representing the frequency with which a given term appeared in the text published at that time. The vector is represented as $X _ { i } = ( x _ { i , 1 } , x _ { i , 2 } , \ldots , x _ { i , T } ) ^ { \prime }$ , where $i = 1 , \ldots , N$ . Subsequently, these data are normalized. We convert to a $T \times N$ panel dataset, comprising variables $X _ { i }$ , $i = 1 , \ldots , N$ , where the cross-sectional dimension is greater than the number of time periods. Because of $N > > T$ , simple regression methods may lead to a dimensionality catastrophe and overfi tting. Consequently, appropriate dimensionality reduction techniques must be adopted.

Our analysis employs monthly returns as the target variable, with sentiment features generated from fi nancial news aggregated over the same monthly horizon. This results in long-form text corpora per period, even exceeding thousands of words. In such cases, it is better suited to global term-statistical approaches such as TF-IDF, which can handle texts of any length, require far less resources to train and deploy. Moreover, contextual embedding models such as BERT and FinBERT accept a maximum input sequence length of 512 tokens, beyond which truncation or segmentation is required. Processing entire corpora with these models would demand complex hierarchical strategies or sentence-by-sentence embeddings followed by pooling or aggregation, introducing significant computational cost and engineering complexity. Given the scale of our dataset, applying TF-IDF is still more cost-eff ective [38]. In this study, we prioritized computational efficiency and scalability in the sentiment representation stage in order to allocate greater modeling complexity and resources to the ensemble. In the next subsection, we propose an attention-PCA method that constructs supervised sentiment indices to further improve the representation quality of TF-IDF features.

A priori knowledge of term importance is introduced into the term sampling process, reassigning the attention weights according to the importance of variables. Furthermore, sentiment indices with varying characteristics can be generated to more accurately capture stock fl uctuations under different market conditions.

The fi rst principal component is selected through attention-PCA to construct the sentiment indices. The traditional PCA is an unsupervised method that completely ignores the forecasting target information [39]. To address this limitation, the attention mechanism is combined with the PCA method to achieve supervised data enhancement technology. The attention-PCA technique allows the supervised extraction of the most relevant infl uences that affect the stock return series, thereby reducing overfi tting and achieving higher forecasting accuracy and better generalization.

# 2.2. Ensemble forecasting based on diversity enhancement

Combining several forecasts from different models to make the fi nal forecast is called ensemble forecasting [31]. The approaches based on ensemble learning can be divided into two categories: combine model and combine data. By integrating the advantages of different base models, ensemble forecasting can effectively reduce the possible bias and variance of a single model to improve the accuracy and stability of forecasting [40].

Simple mathematical operator is the simplest and most intuitive ensemble strategy. The commonly used methods include simple average, median and truncated mean [41]. The truncated mean method is to calculate the average value by removing two extreme predicted values, in which one extreme value should be removed from each side. Forecasting error is one of the most important criteria to judge the performance of the model. It is reasonable to assign higher weights to base models with smaller forecasting errors, which constitutes the core principle of the forecasting performance weighting method. With the continuous advancement of AI techniques, the use of optimization algorithms to determine the optimal model weights has become increasingly prevalent. The artifi cial bee colony (ABC) algorithm is proposed by Karaboga [42], which is a heuristic intelligent optimization algorithm. Some scholars have obtained the best weighting vector through ABC technology [43].

# 2.2.1. Ensemble diversity

One of the primary reasons for the success of ensemble forecasting is the increased diversity among base models, as also emphasized by Dietterich [29]. Ensemble diversity refers to the differences between individual models. For the ensemble to achieve superior performance, it is essential that the base models exhibit sufficient diversity. The effectiveness of ensemble forecasting largely hinges on an appropriate balance between the accuracy and diversity of its constituent models. However, generating base models that are both accurate and diverse remains a significant challenge [32].

Diversity measures are usually used to evaluate the degree of difference between different models in an ensemble model. However, there is still no widely accepted measurement method for diversity, making it difficult to accurately characterize and measure [31]. This study introduces the concepts of bias-variance-covariance decomposition and double-fault measurement as tools for assessing the diversity of models.

Bias-variance-covariance decomposition is a method for analyzing model errors [44], and is particularly suitable for diversity assessment in ensemble learning. Bias-variance-covariance decomposition decomposes the total error of the model into three parts: bias, variance, and covariance. Bias represents the difference between the expected value predicted by the model and the true value, that is, the model’s fi tting ability. Variance represents the degree of variation in the model’s predicted values on different training data sets, that is, the volatility of the model. Covariance represents the degree of coordinated variation in the predicted values between models, that is, the correlation between models. Assuming that the individual models are combined with equal weights, it follows that:

$$

# 2.2.2. Semi-heterogeneous ensemble forecasting

In ensemble forecasting, diversifi ed base model generation mechanism, diversity measurement method and efficient combination strategy are three major research hotspots. In response to these challenges, this study aims to generate base models that are both accurate and diverse to minimize the generalization error of the ensemble; to extend and innovate diversity metrics applicable to regression tasks; and to explore ensemble strategies that effectively balance forecasting accuracy and diversity.

To enhance the robustness and generalization capability of stock return forecasting models, we propose a semi-heterogeneous ensemble forecasting framework that integrates both algorithmic diversity and data-driven perturbation mechanisms. Fig. 1 illustrates the overall framework of the proposed semi-heterogeneous ensemble forecasting model. The “Att-Model” in the framework fi gure represents the forecasting model based on attention-PCA technique. The framework consists of three main components: construction of supervised sentiment indices, generation of diversifi ed base forecasting model pool and ensemble forecasting. This framework incorporates characteristics of both homogeneous and heterogeneous ensembles, and enhances ensemble diversity while maintaining high predictive performance. In the proposed framework, base models are constructed using multiple algorithms, combined with perturbations to parameters and input features within each algorithm. Therefore, it integrates the advantages of both ensemble types: it does not require a large model pool as in fully heterogeneous ensembles, yet it achieves higher diversity than typical homogeneous ensembles.

By incorporating both inter-model heterogeneity and intra-model variation, the proposed framework achieves a balance between predictive accuracy and ensemble diversity. Furthermore, a weighted combination strategy is employed to integrate the base models, where the weights are adaptively optimized based on the individual model’s predictive performance and complementarity. In terms of direction forecasting accuracy, this section selects an indicator $D _ { s t a t }$ of the accuracy of forecasting upward and downward directions, which is calculated as follows:

$$
\begin{array} { l l } { \displaystyle { D _ { s t a t } = \frac { 1 } { n } \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } a _ { i } \times 1 0 0 \% } } \\ { \displaystyle { a _ { i } = \left\{ \begin{array} { l l } { 1 } & { \left( y _ { i + 1 } - y _ { i } \right) \left( \hat { y } _ { i + 1 } - y _ { i } \right) \ge 0 } \\ { 0 } & { \mathrm { o t h e r s } } \end{array} \right. } } \end{array}
$$

where $n$ is the number of samples in the test set, $y _ { i }$ is the true value, and $\hat { y } _ { i }$ is the predicted value.

# 3.3.2. Statistic test

To statistically evaluate whether the predictive performance differences between models are significant, this study applies the Diebold-Mariano (DM) test [47], a widely used approach for comparing the forecast accuracy of two competing models. The DM test is designed to compare the predictive accuracy of two forecasts by analyzing the statistical properties of their error differentials over time. The DM test is employed to assess whether two competing forecasting models exhibit statistically equivalent predictive accuracy, based on the null hypothesis that their forecast errors are equally precise.

The DM test evaluates whether the mean of the loss differentials, derived from the error series $e _ { 1 t }$ and $e _ { 2 t }$ (where $t = 1 , 2 , \dots , T )$ , is statistically indistinguishable from zero. The loss function $L$ of the error series is defi ned as follows:

$$
L ( e _ { i t } ) = \sum _ { t = 1 } ^ { T } \left| e _ { i t } \right| = \sum _ { t = 1 } ^ { T } \left| y _ { t } - \hat { y } _ { i t } \right| , i = 1 , 2
$$

where $y _ { i t }$ is the predicted value of the $i$ -th model at time $t$ , and $e _ { i t }$ is the forecast error from Model $i ,$ .

The DM test is based on the loss differential between forecasts from two models. For each time point $t$ , the loss differential is defi ned as:

$$
d _ { t } = L ( e _ { 1 t } ) - L ( e _ { 2 t } )
$$

$$

where $S ^ { 2 }$ is the variance estimation of $d _ { t }$ . In this study, the attention-PCA based model is designated as Model 1, and the traditional PCA based model serves as Model 2. # 4.2. Posteriori interpretation of ensemble models

The description and interpretation of machine learning models represents a challenging task. Consequently, there is a gradual shift toward interpretable machine learning, which emerges as a significant research focus. Among the interpretable AI tools that can be employed for this purpose is the Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) method, which has been utilized to elucidate the complexities of machine learning models [48]. The SHAP method generates contribution scores, which can be understood as a means of quantifying the extent to which each feature contributes to the predicted outcome. In other words, Shapley values are used to explain the outputs of machine learning models. These values are based on game-theoretic concepts and provide a way to quantify the extent to which each feature in a given model contributes to the predicted outcome [48]. This method bridges the gap between accuracy and interpretability. The SHAP method assigns an approximate Shapley value to each feature. This is the contribution of the feature value to the difference between the actual prediction and the average prediction. Features with larger absolute SHAP values are therefore deemed to be more important. The SHAP value is a method for interpreting the forecasting results of a machine learning model. It is particularly applicable to black-box models such as deep learning. The range of SHAP values is limited by the forecasting range, which takes into account the interdependence of features.

![](images/f2691e90e49d748a95f974d71933381d4ef6a5866e152a416e1d6e6c47838721.jpg)  
Fig. 12. Double-fault measurement of BPNN class based models based on traditional PCA.

![](images/6c58932cd10a966eb36451bff54560d487dbf8ddde41d026151849e6c4c46b1e.jpg)  
Fig. 13. Double-fault measurement of all class based models based on attention-PCA.

The fundamental premise of the SHAP value is to ascertain the infl uence of each feature on the forecasting by considering all potential combinations of features. This entails evaluating the impact of including or excluding a given feature on the forecasting, and subsequently averaging these outcomes across all potential combinations of features. In particular, the SHAP value is calculated by randomly permuting each sample in the dataset. In order to calculate the SHAP value of a specifi c feature in a given prediction, the feature must fi rst be randomly permuted. Subsequently, the forecasting for the corresponding sample is calculated. The mean of the SHAP value of the feature in question for all samples can be obtained by computing the random permutation and forecasting results several times. Calculating the SHAP value allows the positive and negative contribution of each feature to the forecasting result to be determined. A positive value signifi es that the feature contributes positively to the likelihood of the forecasting outcome, while a negative value implies a diminishing effect. By ranking the contribution of the features, the most important features for the forecasting result can be identifi ed.

![](images/e0417c3ef8b423ec87fca2d614c2074d69ada1388905e5f94e53c7d62e277ccd.jpg)  
Fig. 14. Double-fault measurement of all class based models based on traditional PCA.

The formula for the SHAP method can be expressed as:

$$
\phi _ { i } = \sum _ { S \subseteq N \setminus \{ i \} } { \frac { | S | ! ( | N | - | S | - 1 ) ! } { | N | ! } } \left[ f ( S \cup \{ i \} ) - f ( S ) \right]
$$

where $\phi _ { i }$ denotes the SHAP value of feature $i$ , $N$ is the set of features, $f ( S )$ denotes the predicted output of the model on the feature set $S$ , $| S |$ denotes the size of the set of features, and $S \cup \{ i \}$ denotes the set to which the feature ?? has been added to the feature set $S$ .

In this section, the concept of SHAP values is introduced to analyze the contribution of each base model to the forecasting performance of the ensemble model. Specifically, we replace the notion of “features” with “base models” and treat the “ensemble model” as the target model under analysis. SHAP values are employed to quantify the individual contributions of the base models to the ensemble forecasting, thereby identifying which base models are most infl uential to the ensemble forecasting outcomes. In the preceding section, the fi nal forecasting result of the ensemble method based on the ABC strategy is the average of 100 results. Consequently, it is not possible to ascertain the specifi c weight of each base model relative to the ensemble model. However, the weight of the base model can be approximately determined by calculating the SHAP value.

In terms of SHAP values, it can also be concluded that the diversity of forecasting models based on attention-PCA is greater.

Table 10 presents specifi c sparsity analyses of the SHAP value data for each type of scenario, facilitating a more intuitive comparison of the degree of dispersion or homogeneity of the distribution of SHAP values. The coefficient of dispersion is equal to the standard deviation divided by the arithmetic mean. A large coefficient of dispersion indicates a large degree of data dispersion, whereas a small coefficient of dispersion indicates a small degree of data dispersion.

The results provided by the three sparsity metrics (variance, standard deviation, and coefficient of dispersion) in Table 10 are consistent with the conclusions derived from the results presented in Figs. 17 to 28, where all the three sparsity metrics are based on the premise that the base model of the attention-PCA constitutes an ensemble model that is smaller, resulting in a more uniform distribution of SHAP values. Table 10 also demonstrates that the distribution of SHAP values for the ensemble model comprising the attention-PCA-based model is more uniform, whereas the distribution of SHAP values for the ensemble model based on the traditional PCA is more sparse. This observation is consistent across different model kinds, including those based on the ABC strategy or the simple average ensemble method. It can therefore be inferred from the SHAP values that the diversity of attention-PCA-based forecasting models is greater.

# 5. Conclusion

This study proposes a semi-heterogeneous ensemble approach based on sentiment analysis to forecast the $S \& P 5 0 0$ returns. In particular, a variable perturbation-based attention-PCA technique is put forth as a means of identifying the significance of factors associated with stock returns. The attention-PCA technique is employed to extract pivotal information from the sentiment lexicon and construct supervised sentiment indices, thereby enhancing the diversity of the training data. Furthermore, the combination of multiple attention-PCA techniques based on variable perturbations with diverse forecasting algorithms results in the generation of a multitude of base models. These base models are subsequently integrated with multiple ensemble strategies to construct a semi-heterogeneous ensemble model for S&P 500 returns forecasting.

The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed semi-heterogeneous ensemble forecasting approach yields more accurate forecasts. Furthermore, the attention-PCA-based model outperforms the traditional PCA-based model in forecasting performance. With regard to the individual models, the sentiment index constructed using positive and negative composite factors in the LASSO attention-PCA based MLR model is identifi ed as the most effective model in terms of the $R _ { O O S } ^ { 2 }$ indicator. The forecasting accuracy and diversity of the attention-PCA based model are superior to those of the traditional PCA-based model, thereby enhancing the diversity among individual predictions and thus improving the accuracy of the ensemble model. The ensemble strategy based on the ABC algorithm demonstrates superior performance compared to other benchmark ensemble strategies. The ABC-based ensemble achieves the most accurate forecasting, whether based on traditional PCA or attention-PCA technique.

It cannot be denied that there are some limitations in the experiment. Traditional media may raise concerns regarding timeliness and representativeness. As part of our next-stage research plan, we intend to compare multiple sentiment sources, including Twitter, Reddit, and StockTwits, alongside traditional media, to assess each contributes to forecasting accuracy at different temporal scales (daily, weekly, monthly). Explore hybrid sentiment models that combine curated news data with social media inputs, using techniques such as multi-source attention networks or adaptive weighting schemes. Investigate whether news-media sentiment leads or lags market movements relative to social media sentiment, particularly around major macroeconomic events.

Specifically, we will investigate their predictive performance, scalability, and integration feasibility under monthly, weekly, and daily data granularities. This multi-frequency comparison will help identify the most appropriate representation strategy for different forecasting horizons.
