ABSTRACT Accurate forecasting of the treasury bond market is beneficial for financial institutions to formulate investment research strategies and for national managers to build a modern financial system. This paper integrates the ideas of improved multivariate time series sampling and deep learning prediction model structure optimization, and proposes an optimized deep learning model framework under the LASSOSMLR-PCA machine learning method. Through the LASSO and SMLR methods, the multicollinearity of the multivariate time series is reduced and the variables with insignificant correlation coefficients are eliminated. Then, the PCA method is used for dimensionality reduction and reconstruction, and finally, the LSTM deep learning model with Bayesian optimized hyperparameters is used to achieve rolling time prediction of the treasury bond market yield price. The empirical results show that the optimized deep learning model performs excellently in terms of evaluation indicators for treasury bond yield price forecasting, with accurate curve fitting, efficient model structure, and stable and effective practical application.

# I. INTRODUCTION

With the rapid development of the China’s treasury bond market and the implementation of interest rate marketization policies, treasury bonds have gradually become the main investment target for fixed income investments by financial institutions in China. The yield curve of treasury bonds can fully reflect the supply and demand relationship in the entire market, significantly enhancing the leading predictive ability for macroeconomic indicators and effectively reflecting the operation of the national economy [1], [2]. General Secretary Xi Jinping’s important speech on financial work and the decisions made at various meetings have clearly required to accelerate the construction of a modern financial system with Chinese characteristics, strengthen the construction of the bond market system, improve the coordination of financial infrastructure, and enhance the market-oriented pricing capability and market resilience of the bond market. During this important period, researching and exploring the prediction of treasury bond yield prices has important reference value for Chinese national leaders to formulate reasonable and appropriate fiscal policies, financial institutions to execute rational and efficient bond investment strategies, and the bond market to improve market-oriented pricing efficiency and fund allocation efficiency.

At present, academic research on the application of treasury bond yield forecasting is primarily categorized into two methodologies: traditional econometric statistics and artificial intelligence (AI). Before the maturity of artificial intelligence methods, many researchers used various traditional econometric statistical methods to perform linear fitting and forecasting of financial time series. These methods include Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) [3], Generalized Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity (GARCH) [4], [5], no-arbitrage Nelson-Siegel model [6], [7], [8], Bayesian Vector Autoregression (BVAR) [9], TimeVarying Parameters Vector Auto-regression(TVP-VAR) [10], [11], [12], and Heterogeneous Auto-regressive model (HAR) [13], [14], among others. However, these methods are predominantly applied under the assumptions of low noise, linearity, and stationarity, whereas treasury bond yield data are characterized by high noise, nonlinearity, and significant volatility. Consequently, the application of the aforementioned methods to treasury bond yield forecasting may result in lower prediction accuracy. With recent innovations and breakthroughs in computer technology and server hardware [15], [16], AI has gradually taken a prominent role in the research of financial time series forecasting [17], [18]. Compared to traditional econometric statistical methods, the advantages of machine learning methods in the field of artificial intelligence mainly include the following points: (1) Without strict prior assumptions, these methods can discover potentially valuable information and patterns from existing financial time series, thereby significantly improving the predictive performance of models [19]; (2) They possess strong generalization capabilities, enabling them to make accurate predictions on new data beyond the training dataset, especially when dealing with financial data that exhibits distribution changes or high levels of noise [20]; (3) They excel at handling large-scale datasets, allowing them to fit deeper layers of data information, which is a significant advantage in cases where time series data is large and complex [21]. Many scholars have empirically demonstrated the aforementioned points. For instance, Wang et al. [22], [23], [24], [25] used traditional econometric methods such as GARCH and ARIMA, alongside various machine learning models from the field of artificial intelligence, including Random Forest (RF), Support Vector Machine (SVM), Recurrent Neural Network (RNN), and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), to predict treasury yields. The results indicated that the GARCH model had the lowest overall accuracy in predicting financial time series yields, while machine learning models significantly outperformed traditional econometric methods. Notably, the LSTM model exhibited higher accuracy in predicting treasury yields.

Simultaneously, some scholars [26], [27] found that deep learning models centered around RNN and LSTM significantly outperformed traditional machine learning methods. This is primarily because deep learning incrementally learns high-level features from data through its hidden layer architecture, eliminating the need for domain-specific expertise and hard-core feature extraction. Additionally, deep learning can efficiently solve problems in an end-to-end manner, whereas traditional machine learning methods require decomposing the problem into different parts to be solved first and then merging the results at the final stage, leading to lower prediction efficiency and accuracy. Among these, the LSTM model stands out in the realm of deep learning due to its ability to address the gradient explosion or vanishing problems seen in RNN models, and its unique gating mechanism that effectively captures long-term dependencies in long time series data [28], [29]. Therefore, in the research of financial time series prediction, deep learning models (LSTM) exhibit superior predictive performance compared to traditional machine learning methods and econometric models, gaining widespread favor among researchers.

With the advent and rapid rise of ChatGPT, its underlying artificial intelligence algorithm, the Transformers model, has gradually entered the scholars’ field of vision. The Transformers model boasts efficient parallel computing capabilities, a powerful self-attention mechanism, and the ability to handle long sequence data, making it exhibit outstanding performance in various fields such as natural language processing, computer vision, and speech recognition. Many researchers in time series forecasting have attempted to combine it with financial time series forecasting work. For example, Kaeley et al. [30] used the Transformers model to simulate predictions on technical stock data and sentiment analysis, achieving accurate stock trend predictions over a long period. Muhammad et al. [31] attempted to use the Transformers model to predict the future prices of stocks on Bangladesh’s leading stock exchange, the Dhaka Stock Exchange, and obtained relatively satisfactory results. However, after extensive research, many scholars have found that in the field of financial time series forecasting, the Transformers model may not easily replace the functionality and status of the LSTM model. The specific reasons are as follows: (1) Long-Term Memory Capability: Liu et al. [32] found that although the Transformers model boasts its impressive self-attention mechanism, which can efficiently handle long sequences of data, it lacks an explicit memory mechanism. This limitation may hinder its performance in tasks that require long time spans, as it can only capture vague positional information. In contrast, LSTM neural networks excel at capturing and storing information over long time spans and data positions. (2) Algorithm Structure: Zeng et al. [33] discovered that the existing structure of predictors based on the Transformers model might not be suitable for multivariate time series forecasting. In the Transformers model, points at the same time step essentially represent entirely different physical meanings. These points are embedded into a token by inconsistent measurements, leading to the erasure of correlations in multivariate data. Consequently, the Transformers model is weakened in capturing fundamental sequence features and depicting multivariate correlations, thus limiting its ability to process and generalize different time series data. (3) Stability and Algorithm Training: Das et al. [34] found that although the Transformers model is more efficient in terms of memory and computational intensity compared to other prediction models, it also requires significantly more memory and computational resources. This issue can lead to performance degradation and computational explosion, posing a major limitation for pre-training large datasets with the Transformers model. On the other hand, the LSTM model, with its streamlined and unique forget gate algorithm structure, can reduce the memory and computational resources occupied by pre-training data, resulting in more efficient algorithm training while maintaining computational stability. Therefore, in the field of financial time series forecasting, the LSTM model remains one of the most mature and reliable machine learning algorithms. This paper chooses the LSTM model as the underlying algorithm for the predictor to study and explore the prediction of treasury bond yield prices.

# II. RESEARCH DESIGN

# A. MODEL BUILDING

The optimized deep learning model proposed in this paper consists of three parts, namely, data selection and processing, Bayesian Optimization-LSTM training, and prediction result evaluation. The model structure is shown in Fig. 1.

In the data selection and processing part, we perform missing value imputation and standardization on the selected bond price fluctuation-related data. First, the LASSO algorithm is used to screen effective variables, adjust complexity, and solve multicollinearity issues. Then, stepwise regression is employed to quickly select independent variables that show a strong correlation with the target variable, reducing the complexity of the multivariate prediction model and the redundancy of multivariable data. Next, principal component analysis (PCA) is used to orthogonally transform and rotate the bond-related feature data, reconstructing it to reduce the dimensionality while retaining the inherent relevant information. This allows fewer principal components to explain the influence of parameter variables.

In the Bayesian Optimization-LSTM model training part, the processed data from the first part is divided into a training set and a test set. On the training set data, the Bayesian optimization algorithm is used to iteratively calculate the LSTM neural network’s hyperparameters, obtaining the optimal hyperparameter solutions. On the test set data, the LSTM neural network, with the optimal solutions substituted in, is iteratively fitted.

In the evaluation of fitting results part, four mainstream predictive evaluation metrics are used to assess the prediction accuracy and effectiveness of the optimized deep learning model, selecting the best predictive model. Finally, the best predictive model is used for rolling time prediction validation to explore the actual trading prediction capability of the optimized deep learning model.

Its advantages lie in fitting the generalized multivariate regression model while objectively selecting effective variables, adjusting complexity, and solving problems such as multicollinearity. The principle of the Lasso regression model is to improve the penalty term of the multivariate regression model (OLS):

# 2) STEPWISE REGRESSION

Stepwise regression analysis (SMLR) is a specific variable selection method based on multivariate regression analysis (MRA). Its function is to quickly select independent variables with strong correlation to the target variable, reduce the complexity of the multivariate for prediction model and the redundant impact of the multivariate on the data, minimize the occurrence of overfitting in the prediction results, and improve the fitting ability of the prediction model. As mentioned above, the basic principle of stepwise regression analysis is based on multivariate regression analysis model:

$$
y = \beta _ { 0 } + \beta _ { 1 } x _ { 1 } + \beta _ { 2 } x _ { 2 } + \beta _ { 3 } x _ { 3 } + . . . + \beta _ { p } x _ { p } + \varepsilon
$$

In this equation, $y$ represents the target variable, $x$ represents the explanatory variable that affects $y , \ \beta$ is the corresponding coefficient for each independent variable $x$ and $\varepsilon$ is the random error term. Stepwise regression analysis starts by introducing the independent variable $x _ { 1 }$ into the regression model and evaluating its performance. Then, the remaining independent variables are iteratively added or removed from the model, and the value of the F-test statistic for each regression model is calculated for the corresponding regression coefficients, denoted as $F _ { 1 } ^ { ( 1 ) } , \ldots , F _ { p } ^ { ( 1 ) }$ . The maximum value of these statistics is denoted as $F _ { m a x }$ . Finally, the performance improvement of the regression model is evaluated based on $F _ { m a x }$ , and the best performing regression model is selected as the final result.

$$
\boldsymbol { x } = ( x _ { 1 } , x _ { 2 } , x _ { 3 } , \ldots , x _ { p } ) ^ { T } ,
$$

$Z _ { i j } = ( x _ { i j } - \bar { x } _ { j } ) / s _ { j }$ , $i = 1 , 2 , \ldots , n ; j = 1 , 2 , \ldots , p$ . The main components are determined based on the condition $\textstyle \sum _ { j = 1 } ^ { m } \lambda _ { j } / \sum _ { j = 1 } ^ { p } \lambda _ { j } \geq 0 . 8 5$ , and the value of $m$ is determined, with a cumulative contribution rate of more than $8 5 \%$ . In recent years, the underlying architecture algorithms of ChatGPT and other AI chatbots that have attracted global attention have used many deep learning neural network models such as RNN and LSTM.

# C. SELECTION OF RESEARCH OBJECT AND INDEXES

In order to reflect the ability of treasury bond yields to respond to macroeconomic trends and the best advantages of activity and liquidity in the secondary market, this study selects the ten-year treasury bond yields of the China Bond market, which has the highest investment acceptance, as the research object. The time interval is from April 1, 2019 to November 24, 2023, which is five years of actual trading days, with a total of 1157 samples. The data source is the China Central Depository & Clearing Co., Ltd. In terms of the selection of indexes for the ten-year treasury bond yield of China, this study refers to the experience of monitoring bond investment by top domestic financial institution investment research teams, taking into account the correlation between high-frequency and coherent data and daily treasury bond yield forecasts, and extracting 64 monthly frequency and below from five dimensions: economic fundamentals, macro monetary policy, money market prices, large asset allocation, and bond trading sentiment. Influencing factors index (data source: Wind Financial Terminal). Among them, (1) Economic fundamentals research includes: economic observation data, manufacturing investment data, real estate data, infrastructure investment data, consumption data, inflation data, unemployment rate data, and import and export data. (3) Money market price research includes: overnight fund data, overnight fund spreads, 7D fund spreads, and interest rate swap prices. (4) Large asset allocation research includes: treasury bond issuance data, credit bond issuance data, local treasury bond issuance data, futures data, stock indexes, foreign exchange data, overseas bond data, and interbank deposit data. # III. EMPIRICAL RESULTS AND COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS A. DATA PRE-PROCESSING

Empirical analysis requires filling in missing values in the data. Since this study selects 64 different indexes that affect the treasury bond yield price as empirical data, there are cases where some indicator data information is not released on the day of the treasury bond yield trading day or the foreign yield trading day is inconsistent with the domestic trading day. Especially for monthly data releases and daily data frequencies such as treasury bond yields, there are large missing values, so it is necessary to check and fill in the missing values. This study uses the Pchip interpolation method to fill in the missing data segments with cubic Hermite interpolation polynomials, which can reduce the occurrence of the Runge phenomenon in the predicted data. At the same time, in order to solve the problem of huge differences in the dimensions of indexes that affect the trend of treasury bond yields, the indicator data is standardized using python software:

$$
Z = { \frac { X - \mu } { \sigma } }
$$

In the formula, $X$ is the original sequence, $Z$ is the standardized sequence, $\mu$ is the mean value of the original sequence, and $\sigma$ is the standard deviation of the original sequence.

# B. DATA SCREENING

In order to meet the requirement of sufficient correlation between the input data of the LSTM neural network model and the ten-year treasury bond yield of China, this study refers to the practical trading experience of the bond investment research team of the headquarters of CITIC Securities, and selects a total of 64 influencing factor indexes based on five dimensions of the bond market. Considering the large number of indexes selected, there may be problems of internal multicollinearity, which is not conducive to the speed of neural network model iteration calculation and may lead to model input overload and overfitting of predicted values. Therefore, this study first uses Lasso regression to remove internal multicollinearity among the 64 indexes, eliminate the indexes with compressed coefficients of 0, and then use stepwise regression to select the remaining indexes for significance. Finally, principal component analysis is used to further reduce the dimension of the selected indexes and obtain principal component factors as input data for the LSTM neural network model.

# 1) LASSO REGRESSION PROCESSING

After standardizing the data, the LASSO model is used to analyze the 64 indicator data points to eliminate variables with multicollinearity. First, after constructing the initial LASSO model, a visual cross-validation algorithm is used to iteratively calculate the number of retained independent variables and the optimal $\lambda$ value when the model’s MSE reaches its minimum. As shown in Fig. 8, the top horizontal axis represents the number of non-zero coefficient independent variables in the model, the vertical axis represents the MSE value of the LASSO model fitting, and the bottom horizontal axis represents the $\mathrm { L o g } ( \lambda )$ value. The optimal $\lambda$ value is further substituted back into the LASSO model to obtain the specific information of the 54 variables selected by the LASSO model. As shown in Fig. 9, each curve in the figure represents the trajectory of changes in each independent variable’s coefficient. # 1) MODEL PREDICTIVE PERFORMANCE COMPARISON

To objectively reflect the optimization effects of the forecasting models, this paper employs a synchronous comparative approach, examining the performance from both horizontal and vertical perspectives. Horizontally, five neural network models—BP, CNN, RNN, LSTM, and Transformer—are compared as underlying models. Vertically, the step-by-step implementation of the LASSO-SMLR-PCA optimization framework is compared. This comprehensive comparison fully demonstrates the effectiveness of 30 forecasting models, constructed from the five underlying models within the progressively optimized framework, in predicting the yield of active ten-year treasury bonds.

Table 6 shows that: from a horizontal perspective, the five pure underlying models without data processing by the optimization framework all yielded poor predictive evaluation results. From a vertical perspective, as the LASSO-SMLR-PCA optimization framework is incrementally integrated with the underlying models, all predictive assessment indicator results show a clear stepwise optimization.

# IV. CONCLUSION

This study proposes a three-step optimization deep learning model based on machine learning methods framework on the basis of considering the actual trading influencing factors of treasury bond yields and the computational cost of the deep learning model. Step 1 is input feature selection, that is, using machine learning (LASSO algorithm, SMLR algorithm, and PCA algorithm) to screen the correlation and restructure the actual influencing factors of treasury bond yields, and maximize the retention of the correlation information of the multi-dimensional and multi-frequency treasury bond yield influencing factors. Step 2 is the optimization of the deep learning neural network model. On the basis of processing the input features in step 1, the Bayesian algorithm is used to automatically search for the optimal hyperparameters of the LSTM model, and the LSTM model under the optimal hyperparameter conditions is determined to ensure that the prediction efficiency and accuracy of the LSTM model are maintained at the optimal level. Step 3 is the prediction of treasury bond yields, in which the input features processed in step 1 and the lagged one-period yield are imported into the LSTM model with the optimal hyperparameters obtained in step 2 for fitting prediction and the final prediction results are obtained. By predicting evaluation indexes, model complexity, and prediction performance maps, the accuracy and effectiveness of the optimized deep learning model are tested. Finally, the model with the best fitting evaluation is subjected to rolling time prediction verification to further confirm the actual application value of the model. This shows the reliability and effectiveness of LASSO-SMLRPCA-LSTM model in practical application, which can better meet the practical application demand of treasury bond yield prediction.

In summary, the optimized deep learning model proposed in this paper, based on the LASSO-SMLR-PCA machine learning framework, has shown excellent performance in terms of the accuracy of treasury bond yield prediction results, model complexity, and practical application. It can adeptly adjust to the characteristics of changes in treasury bond yield prices and achieve very good prediction effects. In specific practical applications, researchers can use the optimized deep learning model to conduct daily empirical analysis and fitting prediction of bond industry data after the end of each trading day, to predict the ten-year treasury bond yield for the next trading day, providing a reference for financial institutions to study fixed income investment research strategies. In addition, government agencies usually base their macroeconomic policies on predictions of future economic trends, and the yield of long-term treasury bonds is a globally recognized indicator of economic conditions by major central banks. The optimized deep learning model discussed in this paper can not only provide more detailed economic trend references for government agencies but also help them obtain more accurate economic expectation feedback. Finally, the model has identified further research directions for the industry in predicting treasury bond yield prices, and has certain practical value and practical significance.

Simultaneously, according to the empirical research results of this paper, it can be known that the optimized deep learning model under the machine learning framework of LASSO-SMLR-PCA still has the following limitations in the prediction of treasury bond yields:

1) Timeliness. Although the optimized deep learning model uses time series data from the previous trading day, this method cannot achieve real-time response at the second level within the trading day. Therefore, the model cannot capture sudden policy information, public opinion dynamics, or economic data within the trading day, which may lead to prediction errors, such as similar prediction errors in rolling time prediction on December 13, 2023.

2) Impact. The LSTM neural network used in the lower layer of the optimized deep learning model, as a machine learning prediction tool, has an algorithm design structure similar to a ‘‘black box’’. This means that it learns and forms a prediction model by analyzing a large amount of historical data, but this process is not transparent. Therefore, practitioners cannot intuitively understand the operation logic of the model, and can only see the final prediction results. This is different from conventional regression models, which can clearly point out which variables affect the prediction value and the degree of these variables impact.

3) Comprehensiveness. Although the optimized deep learning model has included 64 mainstream input data of the most cutting-edge bond investment and trading, these data are still not enough to fully cover all factors affecting the price trend of bond yields, including economic data, policy information, and public opinion information. Economic data cannot be exhausted, and can only be as comprehensive as possible, just as the future policy tendencies proposed in the People’s Bank of China’s quarterly policy report, which cannot be quantified in time series like financial and economic data. Similarly, public opinion information, such as the event of the founder of a Chinese real estate giant being controlled by public security institutions, cannot be quantified and can only be analyzed and decided in real-time during the transaction process.

In response to the above limitations of the model in predicting treasury bond yields, this paper puts forward the following research prospects.

1) To solve the challenge of timeliness in the optimized deep learning model, future research will explore more granular treasury bond yield price data. This includes accessing real-time transaction data from the China’s Treasury Bond Exchange or financial terminals such as Wind, and responding in real-time to policy information and economic data that occur during the trading day, to achieve real-time prediction fitting. Such improvements aim to increase the prediction frequency and efficiency of the model.

2) In response to the shortcomings of the optimized deep learning model in impact, future research will consider using the interpretable machine learning method - GAMINet model, to perform iterative calculations on bond yield data, and clearly list the influencing variables and degrees of bond yield price prediction. The GAMI-Net model improves the accuracy and interpretability of the model by using neural networks to replace the smooth functions in traditional generalized additive models, adding interaction terms between feature variables in the model, and introducing three criteria of sparsity, genetic restrictions, and boundary clarity. The model can display the impact of feature variables and their interaction terms through visualization, providing a clear explanation for understanding the impact of feature variables on the prediction results. At present, the GAMI-Net model uses a simple BP neural network at the bottom, which has a gap with the LSTM neural network used in this paper in the prediction of financial time series. Therefore, future work will attempt to upgrade the underlying network of the GAMI-Net model to the LSTM model.

3) In terms of comprehensiveness, although information and public opinion during the trading day have an impact on bond prices, from the actual situation of the global bond market, the price fluctuations within the trading day are usually mild, and the trading sentiment is relatively stable. Long-term observation indicates that these minor fluctuations within the day have a limited impact on the ten-year treasury bond yield, and there are often many trading days with information vacuum within a month. Therefore, using the optimized deep learning model to predict the ten-year treasury bond yield is generally stable and effective. Future research will attempt to refine the granularity of policy information and public opinion information through binary processing, and incorporate this information into the input feature variables of the optimized deep learning model to further enhance the data comprehensiveness of the model.
