# Abstract

Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) have emerged as a formidable class of artificial intelligence frameworks, offering significant advancements in the synthesis of realistic data across different domains. The continuous evolution of GAN architectures and optimization techniques has expanded their applicability, yielding more stable and high-quality outputs. This systematic survey aims to encapsulate state-of-the-art developments in the domain of optimized GANs, providing an exhaustive overview of recent innovations, methodologies, and applications. This article provides a comprehensive review of various Generative Adversarial Network architectures, including Conditional GAN, Wasserstein GAN, Deep Convolutional Generative Adversarial Network, Cycle-consistent GAN, Progressive GAN, StackGAN, and StyleGAN. This survey examines the important roles of Multilayer Perceptron and Convolutional Long Short-Term Memory architectures in generating data, while the DenseNet121 and AlexNet models function as discriminators to differentiate between real and synthetic data. We delve into the iterative enhancements in network designs, binary cross entropy, minimax and Wasserstein loss functions, training stability, and application-specific customizations that have propelled GANs to the forefront of generative models. Furthermore, this survey addresses the critical challenges faced in GAN optimization, such as mode collapse, convergence issues, and evaluation metrics, offering insights into potential solutions and future research avenues. The comprehensive analysis of various domains such as electricity demand forecasting, supply chain inventory analysis, medical image synthesis, agriculture data augmentation, portfolio management and stock prediction operates as a foundation for researchers as well as practitioners striving to harness the power of the optimized GANs ranging from image and speech generation to complex data augmentation tasks.

# 1 Introduction

Recent research focuses on providing the optimization solution to increase the performance by using the variants of the Generative Adversarial Networks techniques. GAN is a machine learning technique that will learn from the input training data and create new data using the same training set pattern. It can create not only textual data but also images, audio, and video data that are not different from real ones. The GAN uses neural networks called the Generator and Discriminator. The generator in the GAN learns to produce synthetic data informed by feedback from the discriminator model, aiming for the discriminator to classify it as authentic data. This feature leads to the utilization of the GAN model to optimize engineering and science applications. The objective of GANs is to learn and replicate the distribution of real data in order to produce new synthetic data instances that could be mistaken for real. This process leads to the improvement of both networks until the generator produces data that the discriminator cannot reliably classify as fake, ideally reaching Nash equilibrium. This method enables unsupervised learning and can be applied to tasks including picture generation, data augmentation, translation of image-to-image, and more complex modeling of data distributions in various domains.

# 1.1 Generative Adversarial Network Architecture

The components of the GAN framework depicted in Fig. 1 are:

Network Architectures: Designing the generator and discriminator networks with optimal structures to effectively model the data distribution.   
● Objective Functions: Defining the loss functions that measure how well the generator produces realistic data and how well the discriminator differentiates between real and fake data.   
Optimization Algorithms: Choosing suitable algorithms to train the GANs and finding a balance between the generator and discriminator to avoid issues like mode collapse or failure to converge.

The components are crucial for designing and training an effective GANs framework, and tweaking any of these can have significant effects that impact the efficiency and stability of the GAN model. The Generator and Discriminator are in a continuous loop of adaptation, where each network’s improvements drive the other to improve further, leading to the training dynamics characteristic of GANs. This interaction aims to enhance the quality of the generated data as training advances [1].

# 1.2 GAN Model Structure and Variants

Some of the advancements in GAN variants are listed below.

Conditional GAN (Cgan, InfoGAN, ACGAN): This variant allows for the synthesis of photos of a specific class or attribute by generating data depending on certain conditions or desired qualities [5].   
Wasserstein GAN: Enhances the stability of learning in GANs by employing an alternative method to quantify the variation between the split of generated data and actual data [6–8].   
Cycle-consistent GAN: This system is capable of learning the transformation between two image domains even in the absence of paired training data and is effective in imaging-to-image translation tasks [9–11]. StackGAN: Generates high-resolution images from textual descriptions, enhancing the possibilities in text-toimage synthesis [12–14]. Progressive GAN: Produces high-quality images by gradually increasing the resolution during the training process, helping to generate more detailed images [15–17]. StyleGAN: Known for its capacity to produce images with a diverse array of styles and unique characteristics, which facilitates the development of visually stunning and realistic compositions [18–20].

These GAN variants increase the capability of generative models to produce realistic and diverse data across various domains.

The study [21] conducted a survey to determine the GAN overview, its architecture, the evaluation metrics, and the different application areas in which its variants are used. This article provides a detailed review of GAN variants such as CGAN, DCGAN, WGAN, CycleGAN, InfoGAN, and Super Resolution GAN. Finally, they concluded the survey with theoretical and practical challenges of using variant GANs and future research directions to overcome these issues. However, the authors have not performed detailed studies on the different types of GAN that can be used in those applications.

The study [22] introduced an AI-driven framework for accurate anomaly detection in integrated circuits. The framework leverages deep learning algorithms and employs a novel inverse feature learning technique. This technique represents anomalous pixel regions as significant features, contrasting with existing methods that interpret errors through loss functions. Extensive experiments using various backbone models demonstrate robust performance. A benchmark dataset collected from a real-world industrial setting validates the proposed method and is a resource for researchers in industrial applications and computer vision studies.

The study [23] proposed an optimized dual-stream framework for Human Activity Recognition in surveillance videos, addressing challenges like low lighting and complex spatiotemporal features. The framework involves three main steps: (1) A shots segmentation module, trained on a custom lowlight human surveillance dataset, enhances low-light videos and detects salient human frames. (2) A dual-stream approach extracts features, combining a pre-trained Vision Transformer for contextual information with ViT features fused with FlowNet2 for motion information. (3) A Parallel Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory network with Dual Stream Multi-Head Attention captures global semantics and optimizes features for accurate HAR. Experimental results on benchmark datasets demonstrate superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods.

The study [24] introduced the Cricket Excited Actions dataset (CEA), a new benchmark for Cricket Action Analysis (CAA) in the Sports Action Recognition (SAR) domain. Existing action recognition datasets lack the granularity for in-depth sports analysis, and current cricket datasets are limited in scale and scope. The CEA dataset, developed with professional players, includes challenging multi-person actions and key activity classes like Clean Bowled, Six, Four, and Catches. This resource supports further research and advance cricket action analysis within the sports community.

# 1.3 Common Issues in Training GANs

Some common issues in training GANs are described below.

Mode Collapse: The generator yields a constrained array of outputs, significantly limiting variability in activities such as picture or audio synthesis. Non-convergence and Instability: Training may not stabilize, with the models oscillating and not finding equilibrium, impacting the quality and reliability of generated data. Hyperparameter Sensitivity: GANs are sensitive to the settings of hyperparameters, making it difficult to find configurations that work well across different regimes.

# 1.3.1 Challenges of GANs Across Diverse Sectors

Some challenges of GANs across diverse sectors are described below.

Computer Vision: Achieving photorealistic quality in image generation is difficult and requires substantial computational resources. In the video, maintaining temporal coherence poses an additional challenge. Natural Language Processing: Modeling the discrete and sequential nature of text is complex, requiring the generator to produce coherent and contextually relevant sentences, which is more challenging than generating continuous data like images. . Healthcare and Agriculture Data Generation: Ensuring the privacy and authenticity of generated healthcare data is critical, and it must adhere to existing medical knowledge and provide realistic and diverse synthetic data that is useful for training medical models. Portfolio and Financial Data Synthesis: Financial data involves time series with intricate correlations and often non-stationary distributions. The challenge is to capture these complex patterns without introducing synthetic artifacts that could mislead financial models. Demand Forecasting and Supply Chain Inventory: Generative Adversarial Networks need significant amounts of excellent, representative data for training to produce precise predictions. Data might be incomplete, noisy, or not readily available in supply chain contexts.

Designing and optimizing GANs for these varied domains requires custom approaches and careful consideration of domain-specific data characteristics and constraints. Among other techniques, GAN research proposes solutions that address these issues and enhance the quality and robustness of GANs. These solutions include re-engineered network architectures, novel objective functions, and alternative optimization algorithms.

# 3 GANs for Demand Forecasting

This article delivers a comparative review of various GAN models used for optimizing inventory management, demand forecasting, and warehouse operations, which helps to increase supply chain management efficiency in various sectors.

We can forecast demand with synthetic demand patterns by training a GAN based on historical data. For scenarios with limited historical data or complex demand patterns, this can help generate more accurate and robust demand forecasts. The goal of inventory management and optimization using GANs is to optimize replenishment strategies and inventory levels using GAN-generated data. It is possible to determine optimal inventory policies that balance service levels and costs with GANs by capturing variations in demand, seasonality, and other factors. GANs are used to generate synthetic warehouse layouts and optimize resource placements in warehouses. To maximize throughput and minimize travel distances, GANs can take into account factors such as product demand, storage requirement, and operational efficiency.

Regardless of the use of GAN for image data creation, recent research studies have noted the increasing popularity of GAN in the area of demand forecasting in supply chain management. It uses the GAN model to produce the synthetic demand pattern form based on historical data. GANbased data augmentation helps to produce accurate and effective demand forecasts, mainly when the application has inadequate historical data or not many complex structured demand patterns available.

# 3.1 GANs Approach for Electric Demand Prediction in the Power Sector

The article [25] focused on a short-time demand forecasting method for the electricity industry using cGANs. The electricity demand forecasting using CGAN is shown in Fig. 4. It explores the challenges associated with the GAN method for demand forecasting with respect to daily load.

To forecast the daily load, the conditional GAN-based framework is utilized, and it is equated to least squares and deep convolutional GAN models. Compared to other GAN approaches, they achieved $4 . 9 9 \%$ lesser mean absolute error for conditional Deep Convolutional GAN (cDCGAN). In order to train the model, the dataset MNIST is in cDCGAN. Further the energy dataset obtained from Algeria national electricity and gas company was named. The twenty-four hours of the random day in a week and month are considered for training the model. Still, there is room for enhancement of the proposed approach by controlling the latent space based on correlation with the created load profiles in this method. Further, considering the greater number of condition vectors related to climate change and calendar variables could help enhance the model’s quality.

Samples in the dataset contain daily load profiles as well as a vector containing 24-hour loading and a condition vector that contains the date, month, day, temperature minimums and maximums. A daily load profile includes the corresponding condition vector given as the input for the discriminator. The generator creates the artificial daily profile using the random value-based condition vector and noise generated using latent space. Combined with the random condition vector, it will be passed to the discriminator to create a fake profile. A discriminator generates D(Y) and D(G(Y) as a result for real and fake samples, respectively. Finally, a generator generates $\operatorname { G } ( z )$ output. By optimizing logD(Y) to 1, the discriminator tries to identify real samples, while maximizing $\log \mathrm { D } ( 1 { \ - } \mathrm { D } ( \mathrm { G } ( \mathrm { z } ) ) )$ to zero, to identify fake samples. By minimizing log $\mathrm { 1 - D } ( \mathrm { G } ( z ) )$ , the generator avoids producing samples that are easily recognizable by the discriminator.

The main focus of supply chain optimization involves optimizing the important processes in supply chains, such as inventory management, demand forecasting, and optimizing warehouse operations with the use of different GAN models.

The implementation of GANs for inventory optimization still requires deep technical expertise and a good understanding of the specific business context, given their complexity and the need for significant computational resources. # 5 Generative Adversarial Networks: Advancements and Applications in Healthcare

Generative Adversarial Networks have been employed in various medical imaging domains for image synthesis, including:

Computed Tomography – Synthesizing CT images from MR acquisitions to avoid radiation exposure risks associated with CT scans. Magnetic Resonance Imaging – Generating MRI images for different medical analysis purposes.

Chest X-rays – Synthesizing chest X-ray images for conditions like pneumonia and tuberculosis. Positron Emission Tomography – Simulating PET images from different MR volumes is beneficial in oncology for diagnosis and staging while reducing radioactive exposure and cost. An image of the retinal fundus – A synthetic retinal image can be created using controllable grading information and lesion manipulation for diabetic retinopathy models. Lung Segmentation – Generating segmented lung images for COVID-19 diagnostics from X-ray and CT scans of the chest.

These are a few instances of utilization of GANs in medical image synthesis that highlight the broad scope and utility of GAN technology in the medical field. The study [40] proposed Generative Adversarial Network optimization using the Whale Optimization Algorithm for automatic screening of COVID-19 using computed tomography chest images. By adjusting the hyperparameters effectively, the WOA improves the GAN’s performance in generating new, additional CT fake images, which then enhances the robustness and accuracy of the network for screening COVID-19.

A study [41] utilized a feedback-generative adversarial network to optimize drug candidate design by integrating encoder-decoder models, GANs, and predictor models. The framework aims to overcome challenges in drug development, such as low efficacy, off-target delivery, high cost, and time consumption, by optimizing the generation of molecules to have desired properties. The study [42] utilized GANs and the optimization of convolutional neural network parameters for improving lung tumor classification, as shown in Fig. 6. The study proposes a method to generate synthetic computed tomography images to augment the dataset and optimize network parameters to enhance classification accuracy and reduce experimental time.

However their focus is not expanded to other emerging domains in which GAN-based implementation can be a suitable solution [45].

# 6 Generative Adversarial Networks: Innovations in Agricultural Practices

GANs have been successfully applied to various agricultural applications. In agriculture, GANs are used to enhance productivity and solve real-life problems using cutting-edge AI technologies. As a result of the use of GANs, agricultural plant images can also be enhanced in terms of resolution and quality, enabling disease detection algorithms to work more effectively. The study [46] addressed the difficulty in obtaining high-quality disease samples for rice leaf diseases and the associated high costs. The authors proposed a high-quality image augmentation method based on a dual generative adversarial network to generate high-quality rice leaf disease images for improved disease classification performance. The process involves generating pseudo-data samples with an Improved Training of Wasserstein GAN, enhancing them with an Optimized-Real-ESRGAN for super-resolution, and then convolutional neural networks are trained on these samples to classify diseases.

The study [47] developed a model that detects wheat heads with high precision by combining machine vision and deep learning technologies. This model aims to automate the process of counting wheat heads, which is a critical factor in assessing wheat yield and is traditionally labor-intensive. Based on a one-stage network structure, the model incorporates advancements such as attention modules, feature fusion modules, and improved loss functions. The research also includes creating an iOS-based mobile app to facilitate the use of this model in agricultural environments. According to the study [48], tomato leaf images can be used to detect tomato plant diseases through deep learning. This technique uses a conditional generative adversarial network to create synthetic images of tomato leaves, which can then be compared to real images to train a DenseNet121 model. According to Fig. 7, this model categorizes the images into ten different tomato diseases.

The discriminator model receives a real plant image I(n) with a label L(n); the generator model receives a noise and a label L(n). As a result, the generator model generates plan leaf fake images If, and those fake images If are now sent to the discriminator model as well. Following that, the discriminator attempts to differentiate between fakes and reals. When C-GAN has successfully trained, it produces a synthetic image of each disease category on a plant leaf. The PlantVillage dataset is extensively trained and tested, achieving high accuracy rates in classifying tomato leaf diseases, demonstrating the approach’s superiority over existing methods such as CNN models AlexNet, VGG16, SqueezeNet, and MobileNet.

The study [49] designed an algorithm to recognize radish growth stages using deep transfer learning. The primary challenge addressed is the lack of a comprehensive dataset for plant growth stages, which the authors approach by collecting images of radish growth stages and augmenting this dataset utilizing generative adversarial networks with deep convolutions. The study tests five deep transfer learning models and finds that Inception-v3 performs best. The study [50] determined rice seed viability using an improved deep convolutional generative adversarial network called a generated adversarial network based on spectral angle mapper (SAMGAN). Using SAM-GAN and near-infrared hyperspectral imaging, SAM-GAN is used to overcome challenges related to the scarcity of natural aging rice seed samples and enhance data for accurate viability analysis, even in small sample sizes.

# 8 Discussion

This research introduces the GAN structure and its specific applications, advantages, challenges, capabilities, and comparisons with other models. It discusses advancements in GAN variants along with their associated challenges. The research also explores using GANs for data augmentation to improve the training of predictive models with limited or imbalanced historical data. It delves into simulating supply chain disruptions with GANs to develop resilient inventory strategies while considering scalability and performance in the supply chain environment. It also addresses the increasing popularity of GAN in demand forecasting for supply chain management using synthetic demand pattern generation. Additionally, it surveys electricity demand forecasting, which has been improved using Generative Adversarial Networks, particularly with the use of the conditional GAN framework for daily load forecasting. The application of GANs in the fields of inventory optimization and medical imaging, including Computed Tomography, Chest X-rays, Magnetic Resonance Imaging, Retinal Fundus Images and Positron Emission Tomography, has yielded promising results. In addition, GANs have also found successful applications in agriculture to enhance productivity and disease detection algorithms. Furthermore, it discusses how GANs can be utilized in portfolio management. GANs can replicate intricate market scenarios to generate artificial financial data to understand potential market scenarios and develop robust strategies under various conditions [56].

# 9 Conclusion and Future Directions

In concluding a survey on Generative Adversarial Networks, we summarize the significant advancements that GANs have achieved since their inception, demonstrating their impact across various fields of application. We highlighted cutting-edge variations like cGAN, DCGAN, WGAN, StackGAN, Progressive GAN, StyleGAN. CycleGAN, while noting their specific breakthroughs and contributions. The survey investigates the important contributions of convolutional LSTM, MLP, DenseNet121, and AlexNet models for classification. Persistent issues, including training instability, nonconvergence, modecollapse, and ethical concerns related to data bias and the generation of deceptive content, were acknowledged. We discussed the innovation in methodological approaches to training and improving GANs, including hybrid models with other deep learning architectures, advanced regularization techniques, and novel loss functions that have been established to mitigate known issues.

We also surveyed practical applications where GAN technology has made a substantial impact, from artistic creation and video game design to complex problem-solving in areas such as supply chain inventory management, electricity demand forecasting, medical imaging, agriculture, portfolio stock price prediction, and scientific research highlighting its versatility and growing adoption. We speculated on potential future directions for research on GAN technology by mentioning areas ripe for exploration, such as integration with emerging technologies like transformers and reinforcement learning, its potential value in unsolved problems, and expanding the scope of applications across new domains.
